I.

The engineer

Engineering teaches a narrow discipline: a structure holds under load, or it fails. There is no version of "directionally sound." I trained as a P.Eng inside that discipline, and it never left — it simply stopped applying to bridges and started applying to careers.

Wide editorial image — swap for final photography
II.

Big Four

Strategy consulting at a Big Four firm is a finishing school of frameworks — most of them theatre, a useful third of them not. What stayed with me wasn't the deck template. It was noticing that the same rigor built for a transformation roadmap was missing from how capable people described themselves: reduced to a résumé that could belong to anyone.

Most of it was theatre. I kept the third that wasn't.

III.

Founding Audacious Strategy

I left to build Audacious Strategy — transformation advisory for enterprise organizations, and brand strategy for the people leading them. An MBA came somewhere in the same stretch, less a pivot than a second data point for the same argument.

A varied career is treated as a liability to explain away. It is closer to a body of work, once someone is rigorous enough to name the throughline instead of sanding it down.

IV.

Where it stands now

Today I also serve as fractional Head of Growth for a Canadian industrial IoT company — advisor and operator, at once. The line between the two was never as fixed as it's made to sound.

Now

Stop explaining. Start positioning.

If your career doesn't have a name for itself yet, that's the conversation worth having. The Manifesto is a good place to start.
